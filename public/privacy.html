Privacy

Last changed 21 November 2026

  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics. We don't sell or rent anything about you, and nothing on the site loads from another company.
  • An email address only if you add one, to get back into your account when every passkey is lost.
  • What you put in a trip is seen by the people on it. A board you share by link is seen by anyone who has the link, until it's taken down, and its photographs and videos are shown there without where or when they were taken.
  • Download what's yours, or delete your account, any time, on Your account.

What we keep, and why

Your account

  • The name you choose. The people on your trips see it.
  • Your passkeys: each one's public key, which can check a sign-in but never make one.
  • If you sign in with Google or GitHub: that account's id there, and its name or @handle, so we know it's you and you can tell two apart.
  • An email address, only if you add one and confirm it with a code. We use it for one thing: a code to get back in when every passkey is lost.
  • A session for each browser you sign in on, with the text that browser gives for itself (its user agent), so you can tell them apart. A session lasts 180 days and moves on while it's used.
  • Your settings: the color and initials others see beside your notes, your home currency.

Your trips

  • Everything the people on a trip put in it: its plans, places, packing lists, quick reference list, purchases and payments, notes, chat messages, photos and videos, and who is on it.
  • A list of the trip's changes: who changed what, and when, with an undo, kept for 30 days.
  • Photos and videos added to a trip's Photos are kept exactly as your camera made them, including the place and time a camera writes into a file, and everyone on the trip can open them. A picture for a place, a plan, the trip's cover or a receipt is redrawn on your phone first, and keeps none of that.
  • A board you share by link keeps copies of its photographs and videos, and those copies carry nothing a camera wrote into them: not where or when they were taken, not the device. Anyone with the link sees only the pictures.
  • Photos and videos count against the trip's host's room: 1 GB across the trips they host.

When you turn something on

  • Notifications: the address your browser's push service gave for that browser, its keys, and whether its lock screen shows the words. A notification is encrypted for your browser alone.
  • An AI app you connect (Claude, for example): which app, and what you let it do. It reaches every trip you're on, as you; every change it makes is on the trip's list of changes, with an undo. We keep each of its sign-ins by a fingerprint, never the sign-in itself, to notice if one is ever used twice.
  • The assistant, where the site offers it: your conversations, kept with your account and seen by no one else. Each question is sent with what it needs from the trip to the model, through OpenRouter, only to providers that keep nothing and collect nothing, and your account is named there by a code that says nothing about you. What the assistant cost per trip is in the host's export, never what anyone asked.
  • Documents you give the assistant, where the site offers it: documents you give the assistant are yours alone: nobody else on the trip, the host included, can open them. What the assistant adds to the trip from them (plans, places, purchases) belongs to the trip, like anything else you add. We keep each document, and the text read from it, until you delete it or its conversation, leave the trip, or delete your account or the trip. PDFs and Office files are read on Cloudflare, which runs this site. A scanned page or a photo is read by an AI model through OpenRouter, and sent only to providers that keep nothing of it. The assistant is built to treat a document as something to read, not as instructions to follow. Documents count toward the trip host's storage.
  • The plan, where the site offers it: Stripe takes the payment and keeps your payment details, your email, your name and your billing address as you give them to it. We keep Stripe's ids for you and your plan, whether you have it and until when, whether it renews, and whether a payment is disputed; never your payment details. Stripe keeps its own records of a customer under its own policy, after an account is deleted here too.

When something breaks

A page that breaks tells us what broke, and nothing of what's in your trips: the error's own words with anything quoted, any address, number or id taken out, and the names of your trips and the people on them taken out too; on a shared board's page, that board's own words as well; the screen it happened on by its kind, never which trip or thing; the app's version, and your browser's name and version. Nothing says who. Each kind of error is kept until 30 days after it was last seen.

If you report a bug

If you report a bug (More › Report a bug, or Report a bug on the sign-in page), we keep what you write, a screenshot if you add one, and what the form shows goes with it: the screen by its kind, the app's version, your kind of device and browser, and codes for this device's recent errors. Sent signed in, it's linked to your account; from the sign-in page, to no one.

What stays on your device

The app keeps the trips you open on your phone, so they work with no signal: their plans, places, lists and Split, the small versions of photos, and any photo you pin. Signing out on that device clears them.

Other services that see some of it

  • Cloudflare runs the site, its database and its files, and sends the email codes.
  • Google or GitHub, only if you sign in with them.
  • Your browser's push service (Apple's, Google's, Mozilla's) carries notifications, which it can't read.
  • OpenRouter, and the model's provider, only for the assistant, and only as above.
  • Stripe, only for the plan.
  • A site a picture is fetched from, when you or your AI add one by its address: our server asks it for the picture, not your device.

Maps are cut by our server from Protomaps' public map, and your device gets them from us.

Cookies

Only cookies that sign you in. wgp_session keeps you signed in on this browser (180 days, moving on while used), and wgp_who tells the page which account that is. While you sign in, a short-lived one holds that step (5 to 15 minutes), and while you approve an AI app, the approval page holds its own for 10 minutes. No others: none from anyone else, none that track you.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and your trips: until you delete them.
  • A sign-in on a browser: 180 days after it was last used.
  • A trip's list of changes, and a picture a change replaced: 30 days.
  • A record that something was removed, so your other devices remove it too: 60 days.
  • A disconnected AI app's mark, and its sign-ins' fingerprints: 90 days.
  • An error report: 30 days after its kind was last seen.
  • A bug report: 90 days after you send it, its screenshot too; at once if you delete your account.
  • Cloudflare keeps restore points of the database for up to 30 days.

Your choices

  • Download what's yours on Your account: your account, your messages and memories on each trip, and your conversations with the assistant.
  • Delete your account on Your account. Your ways of signing in, your email, your own packing, your memories with their photos and videos, and the boards you shared go. What you paid and owed stays in your trips' Split under your name, because the others' balances are made of it, and so does what you added to a trip's shared plan. A trip you host with others on it has to be handed to one of them first.
  • Disconnect an AI app, or turn notifications off, on Your account.
